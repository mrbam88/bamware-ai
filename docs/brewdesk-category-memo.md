## Recommendation

**Primary: Productivity. Secondary: Food & Drink.**

## The two candidates, honestly

**Food & Drink** — Apple: "Apps that provide recommendations, instruction, or
critique related to the preparation, consumption, or review of food or
beverages" (examples: restaurant reviews, brewery guides). A café app fits
here by habit, and that is exactly the problem: this is where café-finder
incumbents live, so it is where 4.3(b) pattern-matching lives. It is also not
quite accurate — BrewDesk makes no claims about food or beverages. It scores
laptop policy, seating, Wi-Fi, outlets, and noise. The safest-looking choice
places us shoulder-to-shoulder with Atly-class score-pins-on-a-map apps at
reviewer glance distance (the risk `atly-teardown.md` flags explicitly).

**Productivity** — Apple: "Apps that make a specific process or task more
organized or efficient" (examples: task management, calendar management).
The examples skew software tools, so this is the less literal read — but the
definition fits what the app actually does: it makes the recurring task of
finding a workable place to work efficient, with measured data. It matches
the locked name ("BrewDesk — WFH Cafés"), the listing ("built for one job:
working from cafés"), and the review notes. The go-live positioning — a
work-fit measurement database with a map, not a café finder with scores — is
a Productivity story.

**Tradeoff asymmetry (the deciding argument):** if a reviewer thinks
Productivity is a stretch, the remedy is a metadata edit — recategorize, no
new binary, no appeal. If Food & Drink invites a 4.3(b), that is a concept
rejection, the class that killed Baat. Choose the recoverable risk. This is
not category-gaming: the binary, name, listing, and review notes all lead
with work measurement; the category simply agrees with them.

**Secondary — Food & Drink:** honest about the venue content, aids search
discovery, and only the primary category drives browse placement and the
incumbent comparison. (Travel was considered — "city guides" — and rejected:
less accurate than either candidate.)
